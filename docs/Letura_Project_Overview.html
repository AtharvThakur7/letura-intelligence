Being clear about what the data is, and is not

  • Two different periods. The listings are 2018–2020 (historical, from Kaggle). The official reference data is 2025. They are shown side by side for context only; this is not a same-period market premium and not a legal rent determination.
  • Asking rents, not contracts. The listings are advertised cold rents (excluding heating and service charges). They are not current 2026 prices.
  • Independent snapshots. Each snapshot contains different listings, so a rise in the median reflects the advertised mix as well as price movement.
  • Known gaps. About one third of listings (1,269) have no street or house number, so they get no location class. The location PDF extract has 26,205 rows against 25,786 stated in the document (+419); all were kept and documented. The source has no coordinates, so there is no map.
  • Terms of use. I used these public datasets only for this non-commercial portfolio project. I have not independently verified each source’s licence terms; please see the source pages above, and I will remove anything on request.

What the data shows (descriptive, not causal)

  • The median advertised rent rose from €12.94/m² (Sep 2018) to €13.89/m² (Feb 2020), with 673 to 1,113 listings per snapshot.
  • Highest median rents (all snapshots): Hamburg-Altstadt €21.14, Harvestehude €20.61, HafenCity €20.29 per m² (47 to 89 listings each).
  • Flats under 40 m² have the highest rent per m² (median €16.74, 264 listings); for larger flats there is no clear pattern.
  • Official 2025 reference for a Gute Wohnlage built 1994–2010, 66 to under 91 m²: €13.18/m² (range €10.03 to €16.29), shown for context only.
Sources: Kaggle “Apartment rental offers in Germany” (corrieaar); Behörde für Stadtentwicklung und Wohnen Hamburg (hamburg.de): Mietenspiegel 2025 and Wohnlagenverzeichnis 2025.

The AI analyst, and why it can be trusted

  • It does not query the database freely. It chooses from 8 approved analysis tools, run with a read-only MySQL account.
  • Every number in an answer comes from a query result and is shown with its sample size, filters and calculation (“Analysis Details”).
  • It is instructed never to present 2018–2020 rents as today’s prices and never to claim cause and effect.
  • It answers questions like “Which neighbourhoods have the highest median rents?” with a table and a chart (right).

Before you open the demo

  • Showcase edition: the online version runs on a saved copy of the database; the full version runs on MySQL on my machine.
  • AI in showcase mode: live AI queries are disabled online to manage API usage. The 18 suggested questions show answers previously generated by the live AI from the database; other questions use the built-in analysis.

A 3-minute tour

  1. Home: key numbers; click a question for an instant answer.
  2. Neighbourhoods: expensive areas and comparisons.
  3. Properties: rent by size, age and features; listings.
  4. Official rents: 2025 reference vs 2018–2020 asking rents.
  5. Ask AI: click a suggested question and open “Analysis Details”.
